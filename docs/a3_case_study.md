# A3 Case Study

**Evidence label:** all numbers, events, roles, and observations in this case are synthetic and constructed for a portfolio demonstration. No real improvement work or stakeholder participation is claimed.

## Background and problem

Post-discharge reconciliation checklist completion is inconsistent in the fictional assisted living, group home, and independent living settings. Handoff ownership and queue duplication are plausible systems contributors to test, not established root causes.

## Current condition and baseline

The constructed baseline process rate is 61%: synthetic checklists completed within the defined 48-hour demonstration window divided by eligible synthetic transitions. The fixture spans ten constructed weekly time points. It is not a measured organizational baseline.

## Aim

Improve timely checklist completion from 61% to 85% in a synthetic 12-week pilot without increasing duplicate documentation. No claim is made about readmissions or harm.

## Analysis

The sample case record separates timestamps and artifacts (facts), proposed explanations (hypotheses), and unanswered items (questions). Five-Whys and cause-and-effect categories help organize inquiry. They do not determine causality or establish a definitive root cause. See `rca_and_action_record.md`.

## Intervention

Proposed structured handoff form, named receiving role, and one task queue. Protocol content and timing require separate clinician approval. The project record includes milestones, risks, RACI role placeholders, an unresolved decision, PDSA, and sustainment.

## Measures

Process: timely checklists / eligible transitions.

Outcome: unresolved discrepancies at the fixed 72-hour review checkpoint / reviewed transitions.

Balancing: mean documentation minutes per transition, duplicate task count, and synthetic workflow burden score. See `measurement_plan.md` for boundaries and limitations.

## Control and sustainment

Proposed weekly monitoring for eight weeks, then monthly. Reopen review after two consecutive weeks below 75% timely completion or duplicate tasks above 10%. These are example triggers, not validated thresholds. A named operational owner must be assigned before real sustainment.

## Interpretation

Constructed before/after movement is descriptive only. Small denominators and missingness limit setting-level comparisons. Increased completion does not prove improved clinical outcomes, prevented harm, or causal effect. A real test requires prospective governance, data validation, and consenting participants.
