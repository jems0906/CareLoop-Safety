# Project Charter

## Project

Post-discharge checklist reliability (fictional 12-week pilot).

## Problem and aim

Synthetic transitions across assisted living, group home, and independent living show inconsistent completion of a post-discharge reconciliation checklist. The constructed baseline is 61%. The aim is to improve timely completion from 61% to 85% during the fictional 12-week pilot without increasing duplicate documentation.

The 48-hour completion window and 72-hour discrepancy review checkpoint are fixed demonstration definitions. A clinician-approved protocol would define appropriate clinical content and timing; this application only demonstrates routing and documentation.

## Scope

In scope: receiving-role assignment, a structured handoff, one task queue, synthetic transition examples, metric definitions, PDSA documentation, workflow burden assessment, and sustainment triggers.

Out of scope: clinical reconciliation content, medication advice, diagnosis, prescribing, real incident review, real resident data, EHR integration, readmission reduction, harm reduction, and claims of causal impact.

## Proposed intervention

A structured handoff form with an explicit accountable role and a single task queue. The form should carry synthetic resident/family communication preferences and access barriers into workflow design without inventing interviews or implying validation.

## Team and governance

The RACI contains fictional role placeholders only. No person, meeting, clinician approval, resident/family consultation, or cross-functional commitment is represented as having occurred. The proposed sponsor owns unresolved scope decisions. Review the stakeholder pack before any real-world adaptation.

## Milestones

1. Fix denominator, eligibility, window, and checkpoint definitions.
2. Map the current handoff and identify duplicate-entry points.
3. Test role assignment and a single queue in a bounded synthetic cycle.
4. Review process, outcome, balancing, and setting-level missingness.
5. Decide adopt/adapt/abandon and prepare named sustainment ownership.

## Risks and dependencies

Risks include inconsistent eligibility capture, workload transfer to a single role, duplicate tasks, communication-access barriers, small setting denominators, and overclaiming constructed before/after changes. Dependencies include a clinician-approved protocol before clinical content or time windows are operationalized and authorized participation before any research or real workflow test.

## Decisions

Open decision: whether one receiving role can own the queue across all three settings without creating duplication or inequitable access. Owner: fictional program sponsor. Status: proposed, not resolved.
